Chapter 1

What is a drone?

A drone is an aircraft with no pilot on board. It's flown by remote control or by a computer that follows a programmed route. Engineers call it a UAV, short for Unmanned Aerial Vehicle.

✳️

Multirotor

The most common kind. It has several propellers (usually four, a "quadcopter") and can hover in one spot. It's the focus of this guide.

✈️

Fixed-wing

Shaped like a small airplane. Its wings make the lift, so it can fly far and for a long time, but it can't hover.

🚁

Single-rotor

Works like a mini helicopter: one big main rotor, plus a small tail rotor that stops the body from spinning.

🔀

Hybrid VTOL

Takes off straight up like a multirotor, then flies forward on wings like a plane. It gets the best of both.

What are drones used for?

  • 📸 Photography & film
  • 📦 Package delivery
  • 🌾 Checking crops
  • 🚒 Search & rescue
  • 🗺️ Mapping land
  • 🌉 Inspecting bridges
  • 🌪️ Weather research
  • 🏁 Racing for fun

    Chapter 3

    How a drone works

    A drone flies by changing the speed of each propeller, hundreds of times every second. Here's what happens between your thumb on the controller and the drone moving.

    1. 1

      You give a command

      You push a stick on the remote controller. The controller turns that into a radio signal and sends it to the drone.

    2. 2

      The receiver catches it

      A small radio receiver on the drone picks up the signal and passes your command to the flight controller.

    3. 3

      The flight controller thinks

      This little computer is the drone's brain. It compares what you asked for with what its sensors say the drone is actually doing, then works out how fast each motor should spin.

    4. 4

      The ESCs power the motors

      Electronic Speed Controllers (ESCs) take battery power and feed each motor exactly the right amount to reach the speed the flight controller asked for.

    5. 5

      The propellers push air

      The spinning propellers push air downward, which pushes the drone upward. Making some propellers faster than others tilts or turns the drone.

    6. ↻

      Repeat, very fast

      This loop runs hundreds to thousands of times per second. That's why a drone can hold steady in a gust of wind: it corrects itself far faster than a human could.

    The sensors that keep it balanced

    Gyroscope

    Measures how fast the drone is rotating or tipping.

    Accelerometer

    Feels which way is down and how quickly the drone speeds up.

    Barometer

    Measures air pressure to work out height.

    GPS

    Uses satellites to find where the drone is on Earth.

    Compass

    Tells the drone which direction it's facing.

    Thrust vs. weight

    Gravity always pulls the drone down. The propellers push up with a force called thrust.

    • Thrust = weight → the drone hovers
    • Thrust > weight → the drone climbs
    • Thrust < weight → the drone descends

    To move sideways, the drone tilts. Some of the thrust then points sideways and pushes the drone that way, like leaning forward to start running.

    Chapter 4

    What propellers do, and how

    The propellers are where a drone's lift comes from. Each blade is really a tiny spinning wing.

    What they do

    A propeller's job is to make thrust, a force that pushes the drone up. It does this by grabbing air and throwing it downward.

    Isaac Newton's third law says every action has an equal and opposite reaction. Push air down, and the air pushes the propeller (and the drone) up. You can feel this "downwash" if you hold your hand under a flying drone.

    How they work

    Look at a slice of a propeller blade (right). It's curved and tilted, just like an airplane wing. As the blade slices through the air:

    • The tilt (called the angle of attack) deflects air downward.
    • Air flowing over the curved top speeds up, which makes lower pressure above the blade.
    • The air below has higher pressure, so the blade is pushed from high to low: upward.

    Spin faster and the push gets much stronger. Doubling a propeller's speed gives roughly four times the thrust.

    Size & pitch

    Propellers are labelled with two numbers, like 5×4.5. The first is the diameter (5 inches across). The second is the pitch: how far the propeller would move forward in one turn if it screwed through the air like a screw through wood (4.5 inches).

    Bigger or higher-pitch propellers move more air per turn. That means more thrust, but the motor has to work harder and the battery drains faster.

    Number of blades

    Two blades are the most efficient, which is good for long flights. Three or four blades grab more air at the same size, giving more grip and quicker reactions, which is why racing drones often use them.

    Why two spin each way

    ↻↺↺↻

    A spinning propeller tries to twist the drone the opposite way (this twist is called torque). If all four spun the same way, the drone would spin like a top. So two propellers spin clockwise and two spin counter-clockwise, and their twists cancel out.

    How four propellers steer a drone

    A quadcopter has no rudder or wings to steer with. It moves only by changing how fast each propeller spins:

    • Up / down (throttle): all four speed up or slow down together.
    • Forward / back (pitch): the back pair spins faster, so the drone tips its nose down and flies forward.
    • Left / right (roll): one side spins faster, so the drone leans and slides toward the slower side.
    • Turning (yaw): the clockwise pair and the counter-clockwise pair spin at different speeds, so their twists no longer cancel and the drone rotates on the spot.
